# Say It Normally

Write like a smart person talking to another smart person. Keep the facts and remove the performance.

## Process

1. Work out who is speaking, who they are speaking to, and what the text needs to do.
2. Preserve the meaning, useful details, bluntness, and humor already in the draft.
3. Cut setup, repetition, and sentences that only sound polished.
4. Replace abstract language with the actual thing, action, or result.
5. Match the requested format and length.
6. Read the result aloud. Rewrite anything that would sound strange in a normal conversation.

Ask a question only when missing information would materially change the claim. Never invent facts, numbers, results, or confidence.

## Voice

- Use common words and direct verbs.
- Keep sentences fairly short, but vary their length.
- Sound friendly and confident, not formal or salesy.
- Keep names, numbers, mechanisms, and honest limitations.
- Allow a little humor when it already fits. Do not manufacture personality.
- Match the audience. Plain language can still be professional.

## Remove

- Marketing, consulting, and corporate language when a normal description works.
- Empty words such as "robust," "seamless," "transformative," "tailored," and "governed."
- Forced hooks, slogans, fake insight, and dramatic fragments.
- Stock contrasts such as "It's not just X. It's Y."
- Claims about quality, importance, or impact that the facts do not prove.
- Decorative em dashes, unnecessary bold text, and headings that repeat the paragraph below them.
- Summary endings that restate what the reader just read.

Do not remove precise technical terms when they are needed for accuracy.

## Match the format

- **README project lists:** Use a project name plus one concrete phrase. Keep it to one visual line when possible.
- **Portfolio sections:** Use a short heading and one normal sentence. Keep card descriptions to three visual lines or fewer.
- **LinkedIn roles:** Prefer two to four factual bullets that start with verbs. Cover the work, responsibility, or progression without self-praise.
- **Social posts:** Start with the point. Skip forced hooks, lessons, and recap endings.
- **Messages:** Make the ask or update clear early. Write like something a person would actually send in Slack or WhatsApp.

Read [references/examples.md](references/examples.md) before writing website, README, LinkedIn, bio, social, or personal-message copy.

## Output

Return the finished copy first. Explain the edits only when the user asks or when a choice needs context.
